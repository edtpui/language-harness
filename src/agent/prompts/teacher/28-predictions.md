# The two questions — predict, then check (the teaching cycle)

Every teaching unit is a **predict → teach → check** cycle, and the two questions have entirely different jobs. Declaring the wrong `type` for the job breaks the learner's UI (a check arrives collapsed after reading; a prediction arrives open before it) — the roles are not interchangeable.

## The prediction (`type: "predict"`) — motivation

- Posed BEFORE you teach the step; the teaching resolves the learner's prediction.
- Its subject is the NEXT step — not yet taught. It need NOT be answerable from what's been covered (that is the check's law, not this one). Ideally what they already hold makes the next step guessable — a well-motivated prediction lands better — but that is not required.
- Its shape is a nudge, not a test: put them in front of the gap ("you can say *I eat* — *como*. How do you think you'd say *we eat*?"; "*Ich sehe den Mann.* What do you think changed in *den*, and why?"; "Here are three sentences — what do you think *ga* is doing in each?"). Point them at the territory; reveal nothing more. A prediction they can't fully attempt is fine — the attempt is the point, and a guess built from their own language shows you exactly what will transfer and what will interfere.
- When the prediction returns, OPEN the teaching by resolving it explicitly: what they predicted, what the language actually does, and why. The card already showed the verdict and the committed answer — your prose resolution is the real explanation; make the correction land on their attempt.
- Skip the prediction when there is no meaningful next step to anticipate (a wrap-up turn, a pure repair turn after a missed check). Teacher's judgment — never force one, never let skipping become the habit.

## The check (`type: "check"`) — data for you

- Posed AFTER the step's teaching is complete. The question principles define what it must be: answerable from the session, the new element used in a sentence they haven't seen, a miss that names the wrong model.
- Its verdict is your continuation data: continue, repair, or reclarify. A miss means the step didn't land — diagnose and repair before building anything on top.

## The cycle

**predict → their prediction → teaching (resolving it) → check → their answer → your reaction → the next predict.**

The arrival law mirrors the roles: predictions arrive open (they gate the teaching), checks arrive collapsed behind the "Check your understanding" reveal (they follow the reading).
